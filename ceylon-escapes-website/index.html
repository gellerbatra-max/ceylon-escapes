June – September

Escape the Aussie winter

Here's a well-kept secret: while Melbourne shivers, Sri Lanka's east coast hits its dry-season stride. Think 30°C days, glassy morning surf at Arugam Bay, snorkelling with turtles off Trincomalee, and Kandy's spectacular Esala Perahera festival lighting up the hills.

  • ✓
    Arugam Bay — one of the world's great right-hand point breaks, warm as a bath
  • ✓
    Trincomalee & Pasikuda — calm, shallow bays made for kids and snorkellers
  • ✓
    Kandy Esala Perahera — ten nights of drummers, dancers and lantern-lit processions (July–Aug)
  • ✓
    Cultural Triangle — Sigiriya and the ancient cities are dry and glorious this time of year

Questions Aussies ask us

Yes, but it's free and takes minutes. Australians get a free 30-day tourist ETA (double entry) — apply online at the official site eta.gov.lk before you fly. Avoid copycat sites that charge for it.
Tourism is booming again and travellers routinely describe Sri Lankans as the friendliest people they've met. As with any destination, check the latest advice on Smartraveller before you book — and know that you'll have our local team on WhatsApp the whole time you're here.
There's no bad time — the two monsoons hit opposite coasts. December–April is prime on the south and west coasts (Galle, Mirissa); May–September is the east coast's season (Trincomalee, Arugam Bay) — which happens to be the Australian winter. The hills and ancient cities are good most of the year. We'll route you to the sunny side.
Less than you'd think. Our private tours start around A$185 per person per day including car, driver-guide, boutique hotels and breakfast. On the ground, a big lunch is A$5–10, a beer about A$3, and most attraction tickets A$15–50. Add international flights (Jetstar launch fares from A$315 one-way).
You can absolutely DIY Sri Lanka — but distances are deceptive (150 km can be 4 hours), hotel quality varies wildly, and the best experiences aren't on booking sites. A private driver-guide costs less than most people expect and turns logistics into holiday. We're on the ground, we know the owners, and we answer at 2am.
Eat everything — just stick to bottled or filtered water (your car is stocked). Bring repellent as you would for Queensland. Pharmacies are everywhere and cheap. Power plugs are the UK-style Type G (plus older Type D), so pack an adapter.
